##  Acer Chromebook Spin 514 (CP514-3H)

sjvn

I'm a Chromebook user for many reasons. First, ChromeOS and Linux, my favorite desktop operating system, go together well. Indeed, it's [trivial to install Linux on any Chromebook](https://www.zdnet.com/article/how-to-enable-linux-on-your-chromebook-and-why-you-should/). 

Second, even if something goes haywire with my Chromebook, or even if I leave it in a cab by accident, so what? I just [buy another Chromebook, and I'm back to work](https://www.zdnet.com/article/a-new-chromebook-saved-my-bacon-when-my-old-one-died-in-mid-business-trip/) in ten minutes. 

**Also:** [**How I revived three ancient computers with ChromeOS Flex**](https://www.zdnet.com/article/how-i-revived-three-ancient-laptops-with-chrome-os-flex/)

Finally, even if you are a Windows user, you've always been easily able to use Microsoft -- formerly Office -- 365 applications on a Chromebook. Going forward, you'll be able to install [Microsoft 365 and OneDrive as Progressive Web Apps](https://support.google.com/chromeos-beta/thread/199243556/making-it-easier-to-access-and-edit-your-office-documents?hl=en) on your Chromebook. Chromebooks are today's do-everything laptops.

As for my newest Chromebook, I'm going with the high-performance [Acer Chromebook Spin 514 convertible](https://www.acer.com/us-en/chromebooks/acer-chromebook-enterprise-spin-514-cp514-1h-cp514-1hh-cp514-1w-cp514-1wh). This tablet/laptop is powered by new [AMD Ryzen 5000 C-series processors](https://www.zdnet.com/article/amd-brings-zen-3-architecture-and-radeon-graphics-to-chrome-os/) based on AMD Zen 3 architecture and Radeon graphics. 

**Also:** [**The best Chromebooks**](https://www.zdnet.com/article/best-chromebook-laptop/)

The Spin 514 has a 14-inch 1080p touchscreen, AMD's Ryzen 3 5125C, 8GBs of dual-channel LPDDR4X RAM, and 128GBs of NVMe SSD storage. It weighs 3.3 pounds and has a battery life of just over 10 hours. For networking, it supports [Wi-Fi 6](https://www.zdnet.com/home-and-office/networking/what-is-wi-fi-6-and-why-youre-going-to-want-it/). Put this all together, and you get a system that's a good step faster than its 2022 ancestors. 

It also includes two USB-C ports, one of which you must use to power it. The Spin 514 also comes with a left-side USB-A port, and a headphone jack. You can also get it with an HDMI port. For a webcam, it includes a high-quality HD1080p cam with a privacy shutter and a Gorilla Glass-covered trackpad. 

**Also:** [**5 reasons Chromebooks are the perfect laptop (for most users)**](https://www.zdnet.com/article/five-reasons-chromebooks-are-the-perfect-laptop/)

This is not a cheap Chromebook. It's a serious [business Chromebook](https://www.zdnet.com/article/best-work-chromebook/). Indeed it comes with business [Chrome Enterprise](https://chromeenterprise.google/) already installed and ready to integrate with your Google-based office. The price tag reflects that. The Spin 514 lists for $579.99\. You can, of course, get it for less.

## Any phone with Google Fi

Google

The phone in the top photo is my [Samsung Galaxy S22](https://shop-links.co/link/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.samsung.com%2Fus%2Fsmartphones%2Fgalaxy-s22%2F&publisher_slug=itechdaily19598&exclusive=1), but I'm not here to talk to you about the S22, except to say it's a good phone, and I recommend it. But, what I'm really here to tell you about is [Google Fi](https://fi.google.com/about/?pli=1).

I do a lot of international travel, and if there's one thing I know, it's that most [American carriers charge high rates outside of the States](https://www.zdnet.com/home-and-office/networking/google-fi-the-best-phone-service-for-international-travel/). That's where Google Fi can save you some serious coin. 

[Google Fi has two plans](https://fi.google.com/about/plans/): In Canada, the US, and Mexico, Google Fi has two worthwhile plans. The cheapest is its Flexible plan with its low price of $20 a month. It gives you unlimited calls and texts and 5G data at $10 a Gigabyte, but only in Canada, Mexico, and the U.S. 

* [The Simply Unlimited Plan](https://fi.google.com/signup?utm%5Fsource=help%5F6330195%5Fhclink#main/selectplan) starts at $50/month for a single user. It includes unlimited texting and unlimited calling in the States. It also comes with 35GBs of high-speed data per month. Speeds slow down, however, after you use 35GBs. It comes with 5GBs of hotspot-tethered data. It also includes free international calling from the [U.S. to 50 countries](https://fi.google.com/about/unlimited-calling/); other calls for 20 cents per minute; free Wi-Fi calling worldwide; and unlimited data in the US, Canada, and Mexico.
* [The Unlimited Plus Plan](https://fi.google.com/signup?utm%5Fsource=help%5F6330195%5Fhclink#main) starts at $65/month for a single user. It includes unlimited texting, unlimited calling in the States, and 50GBs of high-speed data per month when traveling internationally. Speeds slow down after 50GBs. It comes with unlimited hotspot-tethered data. It also includes free international calling from the U.S. to 50 countries; other calls for 20 cents per minute; free Wi-Fi calling worldwide; and unlimited data in the US, Canada, and Mexico.

That still sounds like a fair amount of money, especially if you're not using it as your primary carrier. For example, [Verizon](https://www.anrdoezrs.net/links/9041660/type/dlg/sid/zd-%5F%5FCOM%5FCLICK%5FID%5F%5F-dtp/https://www.verizon.com/) is the best carrier for where I live in Asheville, NC. But, Google Fi is a winner for international trouble because you only pay for it when needed. 

**Also:** [**Flighty is a must-have iOS app for air travel**](https://www.zdnet.com/article/flying-soon-flighty-is-a-must-have-ios-app-for-air-travel/)

Instead of paying for Verizon and Google Fi for 12 months of the year, I only pay for Fi when I use it. I can do that because Google lets me pause my Fi service for up to three months. And, you can do that over and over again until you're heading out of the country and need it on again. If I need it back earlier for an unexpected trip, I log back in via the website or the smartphone app -- and ta-da! -- it's back on.

I can't recommend it highly enough for anyone who travels outside the country a lot.
